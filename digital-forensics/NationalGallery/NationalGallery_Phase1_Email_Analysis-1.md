## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The 2012 National Gallery DC case involves two distinct but intertwined criminal conspiracies
operating simultaneously inside the National Gallery DC. The first involves Tracy, a gallery
supervisor, conspiring with her brother Pat (a DC police detective) to steal a rare stamp
collection. The second involves Carry, a Krasnovian supporter, recruited by Alex to orchestrate
defacement of foreign artwork on display at the gallery. Carry contacts Tracy under the false

The entire conspiracy was exposed when Joe, Tracy's ex-husband, discovered criminal
communications captured by a keylogger he had previously installed on Tracy's MacBook Air
to monitor their daughter Terry. Joe subsequently reported his findings to law enforcement,
leading to the seizure of all devices on July 15, 2012.

This report covers Phase 1 findings derived exclusively from 12 keylogger email logs
extracted from email.zip, spanning June 28 to July 12, 2012.

## 4. THE TWO CRIMINAL PLOTS

### Plot 1 — Stamp Theft (Tracy + Pat)
Tracy, financially desperate following her divorce, conspires with her brother Pat
to steal a rare stamp collection from the National Gallery. Pat, a DC police detective,
uses his position and connections to coordinate the theft. He previously arrested King
on a parole violation but released him in exchange for a future favor — likely
deployment in the stamp theft plan.

### Plot 2 — Artwork Defacement (Alex + Carry + Krasnovians)
Alex, a wealthy Krasnovian businessman, seeks to embarrass America by defacing foreign
artwork belonging to Majavia currently on display at the National Gallery. He recruits
Carry through family connections. Carry contacts Tracy under the false pretense of
organizing a Flash Mob at the gallery, paying her small amounts for inside information
about schedules, security, and event locations. Tracy is unaware of the true defacement
plan. Carry uses steganography and encrypted files to hide her communications with Alex.

### How The Plots Intersect
Both plots use Tracy as a common point of entry into the gallery. Tracy is knowingly
involved in the stamp theft but only an unwitting facilitator of the defacement plot.
Joe's keylogger captures both sets of communications, exposing the entire operation.

## 9. HOW THE CONSPIRACY WAS FOILED

Joe installed LogKext on Tracy's MacBook Air prior to their divorce to monitor their
daughter Terry's online activity. The keylogger silently captured all keystrokes and
periodically transmitted logs via a locally installed Postfix mail server running as
root to joe.sum.twelve@gmail.com.

During the divorce proceedings, Joe reviewed the logs and discovered Tracy's criminal
communications with brother Pat detailing the stamp theft conspiracy. The logs also
captured Tracy's communications with Carry about gallery schedules and security,
unknowingly linking the defacement plot to the same machine.

Joe reported the evidence to law enforcement. Authorities seized all devices on
July 15, 2012 — before either criminal plan could be executed. Tracy's arrest
and Carry's devices being seized also exposed the separate defacement plot and
Carry's connection to Alex and the Krasnovian network.

## 11. CONCLUSION

The 2012 National Gallery DC case reveals two overlapping criminal conspiracies
converging on the same institution. Tracy, financially desperate and exploiting her
privileged gallery access, conspired with her brother Pat to steal a rare stamp
collection. Simultaneously, Carry deceived Tracy into providing gallery intelligence
to support a Krasnovian-backed plan to deface foreign artwork and embarrass the United
States government.

The operation was foiled not by organizational security controls but by a domestic
keylogger installed for an entirely unrelated personal purpose. This underscores the
unpredictable nature of insider threat discovery and the critical importance of
comprehensive digital forensic investigation across all seized devices.

Key lessons:
- Organizations must implement Data Loss Prevention (DLP) controls
- Privileged user activity requires continuous monitoring
- Insider threats are often motivated by personal financial pressure
- Physical meetings are used deliberately to avoid digital evidence trails
